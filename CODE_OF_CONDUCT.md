# Code of conduct

Runtrue is a security-sensitive project. We expect participation to be
professional, respectful, and safe for people with different backgrounds,
identities, experience levels, and viewpoints.

## Expected behavior

- Be constructive, specific, and considerate.
- Critique ideas and implementations without attacking people.
- Respect privacy, consent, and requests to stop an unwanted interaction.
- Disclose conflicts of interest that could affect a technical or governance
  decision.
- Handle credentials, vulnerability details, tenant data, and other sensitive
  material only through the private channels documented by the project.

Harassment, discrimination, threats, sexualized attention, deliberate
intimidation, doxxing, sustained disruption, and publishing another person's
private information are not acceptable.

## Scope

This policy applies in the repository, issue tracker, pull requests, release
and security processes, project-operated communication spaces, and public
events where someone is representing Runtrue.

## Reporting

Report conduct concerns privately to Baheer Kamal at
`baheer09@gmail.com`. Do not open a public issue containing personal or
sensitive details. Security vulnerabilities must instead follow
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

Include the relevant dates, locations, people involved, available evidence,
and any immediate safety concern. Reports will be acknowledged privately,
shared only with people needed to evaluate them, and retained no longer than
necessary for enforcement and accountability.

If the named contact is involved in a report, has a conflict of interest, or
cannot provide an impartial review, enforcement must pause until an independent
reviewer is assigned. Retaliation against a reporter or participant in an
investigation is prohibited.

## Enforcement

Proportionate responses may include a private warning, removal of content,
temporary participation limits, a permanent ban, reversal of project-granted
authority, or referral to the relevant platform or authorities when necessary
for safety or legal compliance.

Enforcement decisions should identify the policy violated, preserve the
privacy of affected people, document conflicts and recusals, and provide an
appeal path to an uninvolved maintainer when one is available.
